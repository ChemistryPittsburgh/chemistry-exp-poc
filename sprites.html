Sprite-sheet spheres

The same four spheres, path-traced in Blender Cycles as 360° turntables and played back as WebP sprite sheets. No WebGL, no 3D library: just an image and a few lines of JS. Switch the frame count and playback mode to judge smoothness against weight.

How many frames?

  • 72 frames (5° steps): smooth autoplay even without crossfade. The heaviest option.
  • 36 frames (10°): with crossfade it's nearly indistinguishable from 72 at this speed, and fine for drag or scroll. The best balance.
  • 24 frames (15°): crossfade hides most stepping in autoplay. Details like the zipper or the 8-ball window ghost slightly when you stop on a frame mid-blend.
  • Animated WebP: 36 frames in a plain <img> with zero JS. It can't be scrubbed or paused, but it's the simplest drop-in.

Trade-offs vs. real-time 3D

  • Better image quality (path-traced light, real hair on the tennis ball) and it runs on anything.
  • Rotation is locked to one axis and one lighting setup, so there's no free orbiting or light-switch effects.
  • Rendered at 400 px per frame, so it's soft on large retina displays. A 2× set roughly triples the weight.
